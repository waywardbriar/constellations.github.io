Spells

Spells, or configurations, are a process by which a person, creature, or other entity alters themselves temporarily to channel elements and energy to achieve a certain effect. They can be as complicated or as simple as the caster wants, but do require some work for them to well …work. The first step for any trained spell caster is to build a foci. Within and below are all the pieces anyone would need to build their first spell.

Foci

Spells are like weapons or tools, and most people have few spells under their belt. Many spells are unique to their caster; they change and build on them over time. One should keep in mind that they need a device known as a foci to cast properly and avoid unnecessary ameliorations.

Think about a radio antenna catching a signal to play a song; a foci catches the energy and power required and channels it into a specific form. Foci’s act as channels that better sort and stabilize magic as they cast; some foci even modulate spells on their own and provide benefits to their wielder. Foci are built around the caster; they are unique to each person and require some investment of self.

All spells must have a foci to be cast. Foci are generally any item, artwork, piece, writing, etc., that somehow combine the resonances (components) of a spell to cast. The form they can take is practically endless but keep in mind the setting and the GM discretion.

Casting without a foci will result in both a penalty to the user and potential ameliorations.

All foci start out as Normal quality and can be improved with time. A normal foci grant no bonus die to casting bids but incurs no penalties.

Spell Components

All magic is chained together as a series of near-infinite moving pieces; these pieces can be channeled through devices, and a few gifted people can use it directly. These pieces are called spell components for simplicity. When you put enough together, something happens, like words in a sentence.

With the appropriate knowledge, a user can utilize the tables below in unique ways to build spells of their own. The more you select, the more expensive the spell. Keep in mind the components merely dictate the spell's rules; you write how that looks, what happens based on constraints, and how it's used.

Spell Construction

At minimum, for a spell to work, it MUST have a shape, range & an element. This reflects intent in manipulating magic and is the cornerstone of any spell. All spells automatically begin with the following components: Shapeless, Self & Untyped.

A spell's "damage" is determined by the specific foci for the spell, as well as any boosts from feats. The focis quality plus your bid determines a spell's strength.

Spells have varying casting times that are affected by your skill/feats as well as modulated by duration. Most spells last an instant or ten seconds without modulation of a component.

Spells with more components are more difficult to cast without the right training. At base any player can have a spell with 4 (1 target, 1 damage, 1 condition, 1 element = 4) components.

For example, if I wanted to build a spell that illuminates another target for ten seconds, I would use my merit to build a spell that has the following components:

Range: Aura

Shape: Touch 1

Element: Light

I could call that spell "illuminate." There is always a chance a spell fails or an opponent blocks or resists a spell. Like a weapon, they can generally be guarded against or straight out avoided.

Any additional components can help refine and build on a spell; any spell can be changed over time. Components can be swapped and removed at any point for their merit value, as long as the time is appropriate.

Duration

This represents how long a spell lasts. Typically, spells automatically last about ten seconds, the normal turn length during a scene. However, this can be modulated with components. You do not need to buy each component below the highest time cost you want for a spell; simply decide how long and pay that cost. For example, two turns or twenty seconds cost 4 points, and 2 hours cost 24 points. In most cases, it will be more cost-effective to choose a higher duration if the spell's purpose is to last long periods of time.

Afflictions

In addition to damage, casters can choose to implement conditions into a spell as well to help control the battlefield and beyond.

Any ability with an X has magnitudes (or severity); these can be increased by 1 for the same cost up to 2 until you have the proper feats. (Mote Of Power I-III)

6 Unsteady Your stance is compromised somehow; it is harder to balance. You are unable to move unless you take an action to find your footing. Using a movement action to steady yourself.

4 Concealed You are well disguised; others are unlikely to notice you. You can move and take actions without being noticed. Ends if you attack or draw attention to yourself.
