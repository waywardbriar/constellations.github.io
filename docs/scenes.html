Scenes

To establish this more thoroughly, there are three types of scenes. Combat, Social, Discovery. Each has their own unique ruleset that outlines various types of events you may encounter through your time in the world. Below each is written about in greater detail.

Combat & Action Points

By now the process is the same for all checks, players bid a number of dice against another factor.

Players have three action points they can use at base each turn. Each action requires a certain number of points, meaning players need to budget how they are using their turns. Imagine each turn as a five-second slice of time, combat should generally feel fast and end quickly. Below is a table with cost of various actions and things you can do, this is not an exhaustive list, and you can do things outside this with proper communication with your GM and other parties.

1 Preparing A Foci To cast a spell is a two part process, one must first prime the spell foci, then they cast it as a separate action.

Attacking

Attacks are based on how many dice a player is willing to gamble, some attacks require a minimum and these are denoted by the weapon or feat utilized. Players should read and know their weapons, spells and feats beforehand.

Whether an attack hits is directly determined by the other player's guard. They also gamble how many dice they are willing to spend to defend themselves. All creatures and objects have a base guard they will typically use.

Guard can be done at any point by anyone, but risks strain. Some feats and equipment can provide the player with bonuses or “passive guard”

When you win an attack roll, the combatant will receive injuries or conditions depending on how large the win margin is. Only one die might result in minor injuries or temporary conditions, while higher margins, 3+ result in major and moderate injuries and more ongoing conditions.

The player can be descriptive in how they injure the attacked, or the GM can apply the injuries they think appropriate.

Movement

During combat, players can also choose to move a number of ways. A movement is considered any action that would involve your character changing position, retreating, climbing, taking cover, etc.

Move

To simply move across the battlefield during a scene, you might not need to bid. You will move according to your speed, a number of squares. Hazards, however, might require a bid to avoid. This could be active firing of weapons, spells, or treacherous ground.

Flee

To escape combat, you will choose to flee using the appropriate amount of action points. Occasionally you will have to bid against the party you are in combat with. In these situations, if you succeed, you successfully flee; failure usually means you are unable to complete the action or meet a complication. This could include tripping or getting attacked as you move away.

Composure

There may be a scenario where you are knocked off your feet, on the ground, or snagged by some hazard. In this situation, you can choose to get back on your feet or compose yourself for future turns. Unless restrained, you can do this uncontested. While this means you don’t always need to bid, you will have to use an action point.

Abilities/Feats/Actions

Occasionally, players may choose to instead utilize an ability or special feat during combat. These have their own rules and are described within the text of each feat or ability.

They can also do any number of other unorthodox actions, which should be reasonable in the constraint of time of a turn. GMs will adjudicate bids and dice rolls for these scenarios outside the normal scope of combat.

A Combat Round

For the complete breakdown of combat, here is an example.

Process:

Phase 1: Combat Order

Initiative is determined by either mind/body/soul depending on the scenario. Generally GM will decide unless you have a feat that allows otherwise.

Phase 2: Decisions

Combat occurs in order of whose scores are best.

Each player selects an action from a list of possibilities and describes what they are doing. All combatants will have three action points unless an ability, condition, or feat says otherwise.

On your turn you will decide how to use your action points, selecting to attack, move, or any number of other items.

Phase 3: Bidding

Actions that require precision or skill generally require you to bid. To do so, you will decide how many dice to utilize for your action

Multiple 8s: Strain Per 8 & Complications

8: Loose 1 Stamina (Strain)

2-7: Successful Effort

1: Opportunity

Phase 4: Outcomes

Determine winners and losers for actions and figure out outcomes and injuries appropriately. The next player or entity then takes their turn.

Advantage

Advantage is a status that is gained through a couple of means depending on the scene type. It represents a number of dice in addition to your own during any bid. These dice do not incur strain. These are generally meant to supplement rolls and do not account for whether players have specific knowledge in a field or task.

Combat Advantage

During combat, you can gain advantage for a number of reasons. These are outlined here:

  1. Surround The Enemy: For each person over one that is in melee range around an enemy during combat, you gain +1 die per person starting at 2. Therefore, 2 people give you +1, 3 people give you +2, and so on.

    1. Exceptions: For creatures of larger size classes, the number of people increases for each size class over the largest player combatant required to gain a +1. For one size, that's 3; for two, it's four, and so on.

  2. Outnumber the Enemy: If you have overwhelming numbers on the enemy, situationally you may be granted advantage based on GM adjudication. This should be based on the difficulty of the fight.

  3. Positional: If you have higher ground, or you have steady footing while your enemy does not, you may count attack bids as +1 higher. This is only added if the user is in the effective range of the weapon.

Social Advantage

  1. Reputation: generally your reputation is considered the primary counter of advantage during any specific social interaction.

  2. Numbers: In some attempts, having higher numbers may afford you a better chance and thus give you a situational advantage at the GM’s discretion.

Discovery Advantage

  1. Knowledge: If you have unprecedented knowledge of the case, situation, or place based on in game information, you may have an advantage at the GM’s discretion when completing tasks or finding information during an investigation.

Exploration/Discovery

When a specific situation requires players to investigate or explore an area or occurrence, a different type of scene may be utilized. Players are on the clock, determined by the scenario and have so much time to discover enough information to form conclusions and inform party decisions.

Exploration is composed of a few key components. This includes the goal, discovery track, clues, discovery, and setbacks. The goal is either determined by a third party or the party itself. This is usually to discover the purpose of a device, explore a ruin, find something or someone missing, and more.

Similarly to combat, you may have to roll initiative to determine the order of how your actions resolve. GM’s should let you know if this is the case. Furthermore, you will have three action points per turn to determine your path forward. Costs are the same across all scenes.

The Discovery Track

The discovery track is much like the injury track, except in these scenarios, the players want to fill the track. When filled, the players can make observations and draw conclusions or make a discovery. Players in part are responsible for their discoveries and what they find; the GM is there to affirm what you find. The quality of your discovery is also in part informed by the clues. The track can both act as a timer in some scenarios or a threshold in others to confidently make assertions.

Discoveries

Discoveries are various aspects of the mystery that helps put the puzzle together. There are a few types of discoveries.

Shaky

Anything where the logic used to derive may be flawed, there is something instrumentally missing in the information, or the player encountered multiple complications during exploration.

Basic

A basic fact or piece of information that fills out evidence and asks more questions and points in a direction for discovery.

Solid

Distinct and solid information based on well-thought-out investigations, which answers more things than it questions. This is usually based on fact and irrefutable in some aspect.

Into The Unknown

Bidding dice during certain events during an exploration can provide clues, advance the mission, and even reduce the danger inherent in the expedition. Players will interact with items from artifacts, evidence, puzzles, and more to piece together information. The quality of clues/information will depend on the success of various bids, advancing the discovery track.

Setbacks

These are hazards or events that occur either naturally or as a consequence of player action during a scene. In the same way as complications are primarily narrative, so are these. They can turn certain discoveries into shaky ones, and even affect the entire outcome of an event. If players are up against time, this also means that the discovery track can become filled with shaky evidence and lead to faulty or incomplete conclusions.

Completing An Exploration

Depending on the threshold of discoveries determined by the GM, when players fill a discovery track, they generally must draw a conclusion or come to the end of their search. Some events may allow more time and, thus, have longer tracks.

Socialization

Communicating in all its forms is critical to advancing the narrative and changing the course of any play through. Within certain social interactions players may enter into a specific type of scene, called Socialization. Socialization usually represents players working towards some goal, whether that’s negotiation, interrogation, or building rapport.

The various components of a social scene are laid out below. Players' turn order will often be decided based on current sentiment and self-adjudication. You may be asked to utilize action points in certain tense interactions.

Influence Track

In the same way there is an injury track and a discovery track, this is called the influence track. Players should use these moments to decide how they want to present themselves and their party to the world (or person) and act accordingly. You can do anything from subtly influencing individuals, press them, build rapport, and read their feelings about you.

Your interactions during these pressing moments usually fall into one of three categories, negative interactions, neutral interactions, and positive interactions.

Certain actions will require specific feats, while others do not. In certain scenarios, you will be asked to bid (roll dice) to determine your success. Often outside parties will already have some opinion of you or your group; it's up to you to guide the discussion and meet your goal.

Failures here could lead to decreased opinions, strained relationships, and even fights. Occasionally these failed rolls lead to story complications, which are events that can drive narrative but also make the party think on their feet to find a solution too.

Outlook

At the outset of any social interaction, both parties have an idea of how they will handle a situation. Some might use force, others might use muscle in intimidation, and others may lie more, and then there might be a few positively receptive individuals. Outlook represents a group's or creature's demeanor at the start of an encounter and can affect the overall tone going into a social scene.

Sentiment

Player sheets have a contacts section, which shows general opinion towards the player of different societal segments and occasionally important individuals.

Sentiment is a simple 1-10 system, with 1 being disdain, hate, and even rivalry, while 10 can be friendship, partners, or even allies. Most people you meet will hold you neutrally until informed otherwise.

Sentiment is a longstanding opinion that can be changed by certain social interactions, doing jobs, and sustained contact.

Reputation

Players also have a reputation, which is a flat bonus added to bids you make towards certain people or entities. Usually this is affected long-term by your party's actions in and around the world. At the start your base reputation will be derived from your mind stat.

Poise

A stat that represents your ability to navigate social situations holistically; poise is a derived stat that will be used on a number of bids directed at you. While you have sentiment for other parties, Poise essentially represents how easily influenced you are by others.
