Building a Character

This is your story. Whether you embark as a lone expert or with a group of fledgling pilots into the depths of the basin, you play a part in designing your destiny.

Your choice is paramount to the system.

To begin, you should read through the following sections, making choices and filling in corresponding spots on their character sheets. These can be changed at any point while you’re creating your character; however, changes after the point of starting the game should be discussed with your GM.

Background

This determines primarily your outward appearance to others within the world. People and creatures adapt and become the life form that best suits a situation - some backgrounds originated as humans and diverged; others have a deeper connection to the wilds and emerged separately. The following are available to you during your play through.

Names

What to call you? Is it what’s on your badge, from a family profession, a locale, or something more personal? Names can be anything you decide - to better fit into the setting, some suggestions are set out below to help decide on naming conventions. Some backgrounds have more unique naming conventions and are broken down below.

Every group has some kind of unique first name. Secondary names, middle names, and additional titles usually depend on where you’re from and the setting itself. A basic world dictionary is available at the back of the book for reference for some names.

Appearance: Gender, Height, Size, Eye Color, Etc…

These should be determined and entered based on preference in the boxes. Please be mindful of your background when selecting certain information. You may reference the background section for helpful information regarding each of these. It is more than possible to fall outside the average bounds for each group; they should merely be used as a guide, not a hard rule set in this particular case.

Size Class

There are size classes that are merely represented as numbers; a creature that is a size class 1 takes up 1 five-foot square on a typical grid. Any creature smaller is represented as .75, .5, .25, etc. For larger creatures, a 2 means they take up four squares, 3 means nine squares, and so on. The size class should be listed along with other traits of your background.

Typically, characters can move objects smaller than their size class, unless the weight/density is high or the GM makes a specific call. Objects of the player's class size or larger will typically require the player to make a bid.

Vision

There are many distinct types of vision, which will be dictated by your background. The most common are denoted with an asterisk; other types are listed for your benefit but may not be available as part of character creation.

*Daylight

The presence of bright light, either natural or artificial, illuminating an entire area. Creatures with daylight vision typically can see

*Lowlight

The general lack of bright lights, but some ambient or dim light is present. Creatures that are generally nocturnal usually have low-light vision.

*Darkness

The complete lack of light: a few creatures have unique vision, which allows them to make out spaces.

Echosight

You do not have traditional sight, instead relying on sound or vibrations from the environment to determine your surroundings. The world appears to you more as a topographical map.

Heat Vision

You can see the emitted energy of creatures and the environment around you as visible light, making it easier to navigate at night.

Compound

You see the world many times over, giving you faster reaction time but poorer distance vision.

Aethersight

You have a special attunement to various phenomena in the world. You see things that exist on other planes and may be concealed to you.

Blindness

There are varying degrees of blindness, and how they affect you is a personal choice. Maybe it’s how clear or blurry your surroundings are, or perhaps you only have vision in a certain range or proximity.

Mobility

There are multiple ways to get around, and different backgrounds have advantages in different ways. A good swimmer might not be a good climber, and not many can utilize aerial movement without outside help.

The primary types of movement are Walk, Swim, Climb, Burrow and Aerial. Your actual speed and reaction time are determined by your Tempo stat and are modified by your background. You will find what types of movements you normally begin with for each background beside your Mobility section followed by a modifier; taking the form of +/-1 or another number, this is what you add to your tempo to determine your speed. Your tempo is never negative or zero; it will always rest at 1.

Jumping is determined by your Tempo. It will be equal to half your tempo, rounded down. It cannot be negative and will always rest at 1.

Background Feats

Each background will have its own feat(s), which players should add accordingly. These generally only impact play in minor ways.

Across the stars you’ll encounter a wealth of people who have their own stories and cultures - adapted to new and wildly varying climates. Below are a selection of different groups that you may see in some of these locations.

Human
Run-of-the-mill average person. They are largely diverse and adaptable to a number of places and scenarios. Humans by and large are the oldest-known race and continue to extend themselves to all different places and niches.
Naming Their last names are typically based on the location, city, neighborhood, or street they grew up at and stay the same even through various life acts. Sometimes prefixes or suffixes will be added if they gain an official title or office. First names widely depend on the planet but are generally wide-ranging and language dependent.
Appearance Large variation in human appearances, and various earthen skin tones, including from near black to white. Eye color tends to be varieties of brown, blue, and greens.

Shimmer
A group of specially adapted individuals who primarily grew up in the forest and deep woods. They were among early groups to break off from initial explorers, taking it upon themselves to survey the wild in greater detail than their compatriots. An early divergent group began to develop traits that allowed their better survival. These humanoids are lankier than humans on average but appear to be like them in many other aspects. They have developed many features akin to plants.
Naming Their names are derived from their tribal or regional area, usually as a prefix that is unique to them, followed by a suffix that represents their locality.
Appearance Large variation in appearances similar to humans, various earthen skin tones including from near black to white with soft green offsets. Their skin can appear slightly translucent in certain light. Eye color tends to be varieties of brown, blue, yellow, and green. They have longer limbs and tend to be taller than humans on average. Their hair is generally various loose vines, which may indicate the region they hail from; some use braiding and hair ornamentation as key parts of their identity.

Hardy Gut Background Your stomach is more accustomed to eating things direct from nature, making you less susceptible to various foodborne illnesses and other related conditions.

Giants

A group of varied humanoid individuals adapted to the rugged hills and mountains. Their origin is of workers and miners who spent long periods of time away, working and toiling to ensure that their community had access to critical metals and minerals.

Most still spend most of their lives in these rocky areas. Growing up, their group's bond becomes more important to them than anything.
Naming They tend to go by last name first, which often represents the mountain, hill, or range they belong to. A group is more important than an individual; often many are reluctant to give a first name until they trust someone as if they were a member of their own band.
Appearance Largely broader and more muscular than humans on average. They have a range of earthen skin tones, including blues, browns, blacks, greens, and reds. Eye colors tend to be browns, greens, yellows, greys, and reds. They typically keep their heads shaved. Many develop scarring across their bodies, which mimics minerals and natural patterns in the rocks of their homes.

Rodentfolk
Generally composed of groups of squirrels, rats, and mice specifically, these groups don’t have many individual settlements; instead, they tend to be found in large metropolitan areas of humans and shimmer alike. They’ve developed a tongue that uses the common tongue as its base, merely altering word meanings and orders.
Naming Primarily inversion and combinations of human or shimmer names.
Appearance Shorter than the average human, but taller than their wild counterparts. They are upright, generally with limbs better adapted for bipedal life.

Talpids
Originally a sect of humanoids who sheltered beneath the earth, the talpids are a group of smaller individuals capable of burrowing beneath the earth. Their hands are typically larger and usually clawed for digging. Many still prefer subterranean abodes and live beneath the surface of the bustle of modern cities.
Naming Among the talpids, names are based on skills. Typically, someone's craft or abilities will determine how they are named.
Appearance Shorter, stockier, and vaguely mole-like in appearance, they tend to have a wide variety of skin tones similar to that seen in humans, though exceptions have been known to happen. Talpids hands are long and stocky, with three fingers that end in large points like nails accustomed for digging. Their eyes are usually inset and solid black. As well, they are notably hairy. Their noses often tilted upwards, with some having more nostrils than two.

Rūn-hem
From the deep hills and woods to the vast plains, the Rūn-hem and their kin are a deeply varied group. Their people are known for their storytelling and oral history tradition. It’s said that you can tell where a Rūn-hem is from based on the stories they tell you, their words passed down like heirlooms, generation to generation.
Naming Usually they have humanoid first names and take a secondary name, which represents the pantheon their family groups worshiped. These are comprised of the element as a prefix followed by am.
Appearance Broad humanoid torsos with the legs of goats, sheep, buffalo, deer, and any number of other hooved creatures of similar nature. Rūn-hem have a humanoid upper torso that is often broader with the legs of a hooved creature and tend to be harrier creatures overall. Their skin tones are similar to those of humans. Their eyes tend to be brighter and occasionally have differently shaped pupils that largely depend on locale. They typically come in greens, browns, yellows, blues, and oranges.

Raccoon
Unique to cities, these folks have developed clear methods of surviving in high-density population centers. They were first observed in the last century or so in modern cities, having recently begun to take on life and qualities of urban living. Their recent adaptation makes them outsiders in most circles, many sticking to their homes rather than venturing out into the world. As such, the raccoons have a strong family tradition and ties - it’s rare to know a raccoon with a small family or on the outs with them.
Naming Relatively new to names, they have come to adopt other humanoids they grew up near, naming conventions rather than develop their own.
Appearance They appear as if they are upright raccoons. Their fur can vary from rusty browns to gray and reds. Their eyes are almost always solid black. They are relatively short.

Amerem
It’s believed that among the old worlds, Amerem were a unique race separate from humans and their ilk that developed as a result of magical interference in their home world. Some myths claim they used to be fairies, but a mistake stripped them of their wings. Nowadays, these creatures work and live side by side with any other in various settings. They have a strong devotion to their trades and have a general pride in their work.
Naming Others tend to find their names long and difficult to pronounce - which is why many unfortunately spurn their birth names for shorter nicknames. Often these names will tell the story of their family through short clips and words. Vasins have a rich tradition of oral storytelling.
Appearance Short with long ambling limbs, their features tend to look exaggerated in the context of other creatures. They have large, solid-color black eyes and short but pointed ears. Their skin tends to be unusual splashes of multiple color gradients. Some will have green passing into yellow, others reds and blues.

Immersed

A group of people who took the water when nowhere else seemed safe. Rumor has it that many originated from sailors lost at sea. Many have the suspicion that the Immersed are so named because one can’t be born as immersed but has to be made. It is rare to see one away from the water; however, they do have the ability to walk on land, albeit in a slower and limited manner.
Naming No special conventions are observed; however, some groups might take on the last name based on the water body they grew up in.
Appearance Partially humanoid torsos with the lower half of an aquatic creature of different kinds. They tend to have a wider variety of earth-toned skins similar to humans as well as varieties of grays, greens, and blues. Their eyes come in all colors. The degree to which they spend time in land or in water directly affects how human or aquatic they appear.

Fairy
A group of incredibly small creatures that typically fly. They often migrate, move, and hibernate due to their size. There are few permanent settlements of fairies.
Naming Names are typical, though they may be drawn from nature. They rarely take on last names but rather focus on hyper-specific single-word names.
Appearance Tiny creatures with mostly humanoid features, their wings vary based on the location of their birth, taking on aspects and colors that help them survive.

Bear
Deep in the woods, groups of bears have learned to communicate, trade, and build community in the same way as people did but on their own. The bears' society is hierarchical and tends to emphasize inter-community cooperation. They are a magically tuned folk - and highly superstitious.
Naming One-word names, and the shorter the better.
Appearance Any type of bear, upright is the common appearance of the Bear folk. They tend to have solid black eyes and are slightly more adept and bipedal walking than wild bears.

Aerum
Their communities and homes are typically out of the way or high above any normal settlement. Some live in high cliffs in clay and stone homes; others live nomadically in tree-top homes built over time.
Naming Names are given by elders in the community based on the destiny that they believe the child has in the world. These can be action words or a combination of words to represent a belief or ideal they will carry with them.
Appearance Largely humanoid torso that comes in a wide variety of skin tones, from earthen to bright colors. They have arms that double as wings with different feather patterns. Some may have more bird-like features in the face, others more human. Their eyes tend to be solid colors and come in all varieties. Their lower half is that of a bird.

Seeded
After a Shimmer dies and passes on, they often leave behind a seed, which can be buried or implanted in a corpse. Some individuals choose the latter to continue living to complete tasks they left unfinished in life. The body can be of any creature or type but usually appears as an overgrown, moss and lichen-covered version of it.
Naming Usually take their first name in life with the last name of Novazo.
Appearance Seeded share some features with the body they were implanted in. Any skin or tissue is replaced by plant matter of differing types.

Undomines
A large collective term for a number of amphibious creatures that live in the wetlands. They have largely no formal settlements and tend to be left on their own from birth. Unusually as such, they all pass on some knowledge to their offspring, memories and skills, to each new generation. From birth, it’s a matter of survival; many that make it to maturity move into settlements with other creatures over time. It’s rare for any two Undomines to be in the same area at once.
Naming They tend to have a select number of names that they take from the surrounding environments; some take on familial names if they can recall them.
Appearance It’s difficult to create a definitive description of these amphibious creatures. Some appear more like frogs, others like salamanders. Generally they have large eyes of any number of colors.

Lizardfolk
Making up a small percentage of the population, Lizardfok tend to live away from any large humanoid cities and instead tend to live in subterranean and mountain areas, depending on the variety. They are deeply bonded with their families and carry that loyalty with them across their lifetimes.
Naming Generally unnamed until they reach maturity - they are given a name that reflects them as an individual. Up until that point they are named for their colony only.
Appearance Small lizard-like humanoids that stand on two legs, they come in various colors and patterns depending on where they grew up. The colors and banding of their scales are an important part of their identity in that way. Their eyes tend to be any variation of color and are slitted.

Canis
Generally keeping their wooded homes, the Canis are an isolated group that values family lines and is generally unfriendly to outsiders. They are renowned spell crafters and warriors, often acting as advisors and reserve soldiers to any nearby allies.
Naming Parts of names are passed down to children from their parents. Each child takes on their own prefix, and the rest of their name is ascribed from part of one of their parents' names. Which parent is usually determined by group tradition. No two children will take the same part of the parents' names; if they have many children, parents occasionally take from grandparents names for their children.
Appearance They can appear as large bipedal canines with generally humanoid torsos or as colonies of hyper-aware canines that appear very similar to their wild companions. The variety depends on the pack and history of said pack.

Gealán
Small creatures, not dissimilar from insects or humans. They appear as tiny winged humanoid creatures with bright patterns on their skin, which glow at night. They often live in small colonies in caves and dark spaces. Some may live in areas that have minimal daylight, as they vastly prefer the night.
Naming

Restored
The undead aren’t seen as something strange or unnatural by most cultures. They are generally repentant people who lived complicated or criminal lives, looking to make up for the problems they had or caused while living. The secrets of undeath are usually held by small groupings who take petitions from various mortals to undertake the rites upon their death. Most undead have only a vague recollection of their past lives.
