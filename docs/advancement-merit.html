Advancement - Merit

Every person in the systems starts out somewhere. Your worth is what you make it - and that is measured in merit. Distilled from years of debate and philosophy, merit allows you to carve out aspects of your life and the tools and advantages you take into it. It marks your progression and hard work - your dedication. You will gain and use merit over time - climbing in power and through society.

In game the players are recognized for their skill and their contributions - this results in other characters vesting a certain amount of merit in them. When people see your character do things that advance their goals, you complete difficult tasks or the like, others recognize that. It represents your status and your knowledge in game.

Therein your character's actions throughout the game will determine your "level." Formally, advancement is gaining different skills and knowledge; there are no levels technically. Your party will earn Merit rather than XP. Merit can be used to advance in skill trees, build out spells, and update stats.

Your starting merit will depend on the start you GM chooses for the party. You can find the information in the table below.

Starting Merit Table
Start Type Merit Maximum Starting Stat Feat Tiers Available
Basic 160 4 1, 2
Average 300 6 1, 2, 3
Paragon 500 8 1, 2, 3, 4
Legendary 800 10 1, 2, 3, 4

Players are rewarded merit per session and for completing goals related to various scenes, including combat, socializing, investigations, and exploration. Different scenes can have different amounts of Merit depending on difficulty.

Merit must be spent at the end or beginning of a session or during rest and cannot be applied in the middle of some other action - likewise, if the party starts or ends the game during a time-sensitive event, they may not apply their merit. Any other opportunities to apply merit are purely based on GM adjudication.

Merit represents time spent learning and training and should be treated as you purposefully taking downtime to grow.
